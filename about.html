About null Dallas

A local chapter of null, the open security community.

What null is

null started in India as an open, volunteer-driven community for people who care about security. It has grown into one of the largest security communities anywhere, with chapters that meet every month to share knowledge freely.

There are no membership fees, no sales pitches and no gatekeeping. If you're curious about security, you belong here, whether you're a student, a developer, a SOC analyst or a seasoned red teamer.

Visit null.community

Why a Dallas chapter

DFW has one of the biggest concentrations of security, finance, telecom and tech teams in the country, and a lot of people doing interesting work in isolation. null Dallas gives them a regular place to meet, teach and learn in person.

Meets move between host venues around Dallas, Plano, Richardson, Irving and Fort Worth. Companies donate space; members bring the talks.

Our formats

Monthly Meet

Talks and open floor

Two or three technical talks of about 20 to 40 minutes each, followed by open discussion. Held on a Saturday morning each month.

Humla

Hindi for "attack"

Offensive, hands-on sessions. A trainer walks the room through labs, and you do the work on your own machine.

Bachav

Hindi for "defend"

Defensive, hands-on sessions on detection, response and hardening. Same format as Humla, pointed the other way.

Code of conduct

null Dallas is a harassment-free space for everyone. By attending, you agree to:

  • Treat everyone with respect, regardless of background, experience or identity.
  • Keep talks educational. No product pitches or recruiting from the stage.
  • Only test systems you own or have written permission to test. Labs stay in labs.
  • Respect people's privacy. Ask before photographing or recording anyone.

If something makes you uncomfortable, tell any organizer at the meet or email . We'll handle it quietly and seriously.

Questions

Does it cost anything?

No. Every null event is free. Venues are donated by host companies.

Do I need to be an expert?

Not at all. Many members come to their first meet knowing very little. Talks span beginner to advanced.

How do I give a talk?

Send a proposal through the Get involved page. A short outline is enough; organizers will help you shape it.

Can my company host a meet?

Yes, please. We need a room for 40 to 80 people with a projector and Wi-Fi on a Saturday morning. See Get involved.
